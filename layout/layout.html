High-key lighting: the active room is bright, everything not yet entered is near-black. No flashlight, no fog. Health rides under the player so the eye never leaves centre, and the HUD sits on the four edges as chunky rounded tiles. Numbers map to the key below.

Lighting. One bright key light per active room plus soft ambient — flat, shadow-light, fully readable. No dynamic player light of any kind. A room lights up the instant its door opens; that flood is the reward beat.

Camera. Fixed top-down tilted ~55°, follows the player with soft lag and slight look-ahead. It never rotates — the moment the world spins, a one-stick game becomes unreadable.

The sacred zone. Nothing opaque may enter the dashed box. Damage numbers, pickups and VFX are allowed; panels are not.

Contrast law. Growth is magenta, player is orange-on-dark, loot is gold, the world is bright teal and white. Every entity must be identifiable on a phone, in daylight, at a glance.

Between rooms. The HUD fades to 20% and three upgrade cards take the centre — the only time the sacred zone is used, and the only time the player thinks slowly.

Station map — nine modules

One critical path, no branching. Each module introduces exactly one new idea, then the next combines it with what came before. Cleared modules stay lit behind you but the door regrows — the playable space only ever shrinks.

Run target 12–18 min  ·  ~90 s per module  ·  death returns you to the last checkpoint, Seed Vault is kept

Room anatomy — the unit of the game

Every module is built from this template. A lit, closed arena: you enter, it seals behind you, the growth erupts from the perimeter, and the exit unlocks when the last one dies.

Sequence. Enter → room lights up → door regrows shut behind you → 2–4 spawn waves from rotating edges → last kill unlocks the exit → three upgrade cards → next module.

Spawn rule. Never inside the kite lane, never within 6 m of the player. Growth erupts from the perimeter only, so a surround is always something the player walked into.

Cover rule. Cover blocks enemies, not shots. It breaks sightlines and creates kiting geometry — it must never let the player camp a corner safely.

Size. Roughly 33 × 20 m, about 2.5 screens wide at the default camera height. Big enough to run a full circle, small enough that the far wall is always implied.
